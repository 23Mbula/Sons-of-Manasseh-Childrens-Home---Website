Who We Are

Creating a safe place for children to grow and thrive.

Sons of Manasseh Children’s Home (SMCH) is a registered Children’s Charitable Institution (CCI) in Kenya.

Founded in 2007, SMCH exists to protect, care for, and empower orphans and vulnerable children by providing holistic support that transforms lives and restores hope.

Our Story

From a simple outreach to a home filled with hope.

Sons of Manasseh Children’s Home was founded by the late Rev. Joseph Munywoki and his wife, Beatrice Kirigo Thuita. What began as a simple outreach in Dagoretti near Ndunyu slum — sharing the Gospel and a small meal with vulnerable children every Saturday — soon grew into something greater.

Many of the children they encountered were orphans, abandoned, or living in extreme poverty. A large number had lost parents to HIV/AIDS and were forced to drop out of school due to lack of basic needs.

The need for shelter, food, education, and protection became urgent. In response, Sons of Manasseh Children’s Home was born.

What We Do

Holistic care for every child

We provide comprehensive care and support that addresses the educational, physical, emotional, and protection needs of vulnerable children.

Education

Supporting children from preschool through university level and helping them build brighter futures.

Safe Shelter

Providing a safe and caring environment where children can grow, learn, and feel protected.

Psycho-social Support & Counselling

Providing counselling and supporting children's emotional wellbeing and helping them navigate difficult experiences.

Food and Nutrition

Ensuring children have access to regular, nutritious meals that support healthy growth.

Medical Care

Helping children access the medical attention and healthcare they need.

Child Protection

Promoting child safeguarding and protecting children from harmful situations and exploitation.

Reaching Vulnerable Communities

Supporting children beyond the home

In addition to caring for orphans and abandoned children, we support children from the Maasai community who are at risk of practices and circumstances that can interrupt their education and wellbeing.

Female Genital Mutilation (FGM)
Early marriages
Dropping out of school to graze cattle
